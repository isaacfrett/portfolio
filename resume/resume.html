Quantitative Futures Trading Platform

Archived

Sole architect and engineer · overview: github.com/isaacfrett/quant-public

A machine-learning platform that researched futures trading strategies, validated them, and ran the survivors against a live broker account — the model inputs, the training, testing against past data, execution and monitoring, all built solo.

  • Built roughly 700 model inputs from 50+ reusable components, with an automated check that none could accidentally see the future — a bug that makes results look excellent and be worth nothing, so finding one blocked the release.
  • Strategies were scored only on data they had never seen, and the scoring corrected for how many thousands of ideas had been tried — the difference between a real advantage and luck.
  • Trading fees were subtracted before anything was scored, so the bar was "still profitable after costs" rather than "profitable on paper". Two models worked together: one predicted direction, a second decided whether that prediction was worth acting on.
  • Simulation and live trading had to behave the same way, checked continuously — any difference stopped a release. Hard limits on how much could be risked per trade, lost in a day and lost overall, plus a kill switch, monitored on a real-time React dashboard.

Stack Python · LightGBM · scikit-learn · Parquet/PyArrow · FastAPI · React + TypeScript · pytest (TDD-first)
