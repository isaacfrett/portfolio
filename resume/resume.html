Quantitative Futures Trading Platform

Archived

Sole architect and engineer · overview: github.com/isaacfrett/quant-public

A machine-learning platform that researched futures trading strategies, tested them honestly, and ran the few that survived against a live broker account. The design starts from the assumption that most ideas are bad and makes them prove otherwise.

  • Built roughly 700 model inputs from 50+ reusable components, with an automated check that none could accidentally see the future — a bug that makes results look excellent and be worth nothing, so finding one blocked the release.
  • Strategies were scored only on data they had never seen, and the scoring corrected for how many thousands of ideas had been tried — the difference between real edge and luck.
  • Trading fees were subtracted before anything was scored, so the bar was "still profitable after costs" rather than "profitable on paper". Two models worked together: one predicted direction, a second decided whether that prediction was worth acting on.
  • Simulation and live trading had to behave identically, checked continuously — any difference stopped a release. Hard limits on position size, daily loss and drawdown, plus a kill switch. Monitored through a real-time React dashboard over a FastAPI backend.

Stack Python · LightGBM · scikit-learn · pandas · NumPy · Parquet/PyArrow · FastAPI · React + TypeScript · pytest (TDD-first)
